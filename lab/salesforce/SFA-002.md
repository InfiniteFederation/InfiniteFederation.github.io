Distributed Systems

What it means

A distributed system is a collection of independent services running across multiple machines that work together as a single platform.

Your answer

Over the last decade at JPMorgan, most of my work has been in distributed systems. 
The Cyber Data Mesh platform I helped architect runs across multiple Kubernetes nodes and integrates technologies such as Trino, Iceberg, Kafka, OpenSearch, metadata services, and security services. 
One of the key challenges in distributed systems is dealing with failures as a normal operating condition. 
I design for resiliency, fault isolation, retries, observability, and horizontal scaling rather than assuming every component is always available.

Microservices

What it means

Small independently deployable services with clear responsibilities.

Your answer

I’ve spent much of my career building and operating microservice-based platforms. 
Rather than creating a large monolithic application, I separated capabilities such as metadata management, policy enforcement, ingestion, lineage, catalog management, and query processing into independently deployable services. 
This allowed teams to release independently and scale individual components based on demand.

Event-Driven Architecture

What it means

Systems communicate through events rather than direct synchronous calls.

Your answer

We use event-driven patterns extensively. 
For example, when new threat intelligence arrives, an event can trigger enrichment, scoring, indexing, lineage updates, and alert generation without tightly coupling those systems together. 
This allows services to evolve independently while improving scalability and resilience. 
The main design considerations are ordering, retries, idempotency, dead-letter handling, and observability.

Data Mesh

What it means

Treat data as a product owned by domain teams.

Your answer

One of my flagship initiatives was architecting JPMorgan’s first Cyber Data Mesh ecosystem. 
The core principle was shifting from a centralized data ownership model to a domain-oriented model where teams own and publish their data products while adhering to common governance, security, and metadata standards. 
We provided a self-service platform that enabled teams to share and consume data while maintaining enterprise controls.

Federated Query Platforms

What it means

Query multiple systems through a single interface.

Your answer

Federated query platforms are one of my strongest areas. 
Using Trino, we enabled users to query data across multiple systems without physically moving the data. 
Users could join cyber intelligence datasets, operational databases, cloud storage, and analytics sources through a single SQL interface. 
The biggest challenges are performance optimization, security enforcement, metadata management, and minimizing data movement.

Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA)

What it means

Business capabilities exposed as services.

Your answer

Early in my career I worked extensively with service-oriented architectures involving SOAP, REST, MQ, and enterprise integration platforms. 
While modern architectures often use microservices, many SOA principles still apply, such as service contracts, loose coupling, interoperability, and governance. My experience spans both traditional SOA and modern cloud-native microservice architectures.

API-First Design

What it means

Design the contract before implementation.

Your answer

I’m a strong believer in API-first design. 
In several projects, particularly around OpenAPI-based integrations, we defined the service contracts and schemas before implementation. 
This allows teams to develop independently, improves documentation, simplifies testing, and creates a stable integration model for consumers.

Multi-Tenant SaaS

What it means

Multiple customers share the same platform securely.

Your answer

While most of my experience is in enterprise platforms rather than commercial SaaS products, many of the same principles apply. 
In our Cyber Data Mesh platform, we supported multiple teams and business units on a shared infrastructure.

High Availability Systems

What it means

Systems remain operational despite failures.

Your answer

High availability has been a core requirement in every platform I’ve built. 
Our Cyber Data Mesh platform supports more than a thousand users and processes over two hundred thousand queries per day. 
We designed for high availability through Kubernetes orchestration, redundant services, rolling upgrades, health checks, automated recovery, and comprehensive monitoring. 
The goal is to eliminate single points of failure and reduce recovery times.

Concurrency

What it means

Many users doing work simultaneously.

Your answer

Concurrency becomes critical at scale. 
In our environment, hundreds of users can submit workloads simultaneously. 
We use workload isolation, resource groups, queue management, thread tuning, connection pooling, and distributed execution strategies to ensure that one user’s workload does not negatively impact others.

Performance & Scalability Engineering

What it means

Keeping systems fast as usage grows.

Your answer

Performance and scalability engineering has been one of the most important parts of my role. 
I’ve spent years tuning distributed query engines, Kubernetes platforms, JVM-based applications, and large-scale data processing systems. 
The focus is always on understanding bottlenecks, measuring system behavior, and scaling horizontally wherever possible. 
We continuously evaluate latency, throughput, resource utilization, and cost efficiency.

Site Reliability Engineering (SRE)

What it means

Applying software engineering principles to operations.

Your answer

I closely align with many SRE principles. 
We define service-level objectives, automate operational processes, invest heavily in observability, monitor reliability metrics, and reduce manual intervention through automation. 
My philosophy is that reliability should be engineered into the platform rather than relying on operational heroics.
